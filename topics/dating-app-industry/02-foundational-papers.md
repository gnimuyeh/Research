# Foundational Timeline & Key Milestones

Unlike academic fields, the dating app industry is best understood through its key products, companies, and business milestones rather than academic papers. This document traces the foundational events that shaped the industry.

---

## Pre-Internet Era: Computer-Assisted Matchmaking

### 1959 -- Happy Families Planning Service (Stanford)
Two Stanford students used an IBM 650 computer and punch-card questionnaires to pair 49 men with 49 women. This was the first documented example of computerized matchmaking.

### 1965 -- Operation Match (Harvard)
Jeff Tarr and Vaughan Morrill, Harvard students, used an IBM 1401 mainframe and a survey to create "Operation Match." Thousands of users participated during the 1960s, paying approximately $3 each. This demonstrated commercial viability of algorithmic matching.

### 1980s-1990s -- Newspaper & Phone Personals
Before the web, dating options included newspaper personal ads and expensive 900-number phone services. Video dating services also emerged briefly.

---

## Era 1: The Web Dating Pioneers (1995-2007)

### 1995 -- Match.com Launches
- **Founded:** 1993 by Gary Kremen and Peng T. Ong as Electric Classifieds Inc. in San Francisco
- **Launched:** April 1995, initially as a free beta
- **Significance:** First major dating website to register a domain. Proved the concept of internet-based dating. Initial users received free lifetime memberships to build the database. By December 1996, it had 100,000+ users.
- **Profiled in Wired magazine** in its launch year, establishing early media credibility.

### 2000 -- eHarmony Launches
- **Founded by:** Dr. Neil Clark Warren and Greg Forgatch
- **Significance:** First algorithm-based dating site. Introduced the "29 Dimensions of Compatibility" questionnaire (originally 450 questions, later reduced to 65). Positioned specifically for marriage-minded singles.
- **Initial funding:** $3 million from Fayez Sarofim & Co.
- **Growth:** 2 million users in first three years; profitable by 2004; cumulative revenue exceeded $1 billion by 2009.
- **Acquired by:** German media company ProSiebenSat.1 (NuCom division) in 2018.

### 2003 -- Plenty of Fish (POF)
- **Founded by:** Markus Frind, a programmer in Vancouver, Canada
- **Significance:** Built entirely as a one-person side project. Pioneered the fully free dating model (ad-supported). Grew to 15 million users and $10 million revenue by 2008 with zero outside investment.
- **Revenue:** $100 million/year by 2014.
- **Acquired by:** Match Group for $575 million in July 2015 -- without ever taking a single dollar of investor funding.

### 2004 -- OkCupid
- **Founded by:** Chris Coyne, Christian Rudder, Sam Yagan, and Max Krohn (Harvard students who also created SparkNotes)
- **Significance:** Introduced data-driven, algorithmic matching for free. Users answered questions, indicated preferred partner answers, and rated importance -- math generated compatibility scores. The OkCupid blog ("OkTrends") published influential data analyses on dating behavior.
- **Acquired by:** IAC/Match Group for $50 million in February 2011.

### 2004 -- Facebook Launches
- Not a dating platform, but Facebook's social graph later became crucial infrastructure for dating apps (photo verification, mutual friends, etc.).

---

## Era 2: The Mobile Revolution (2009-2015)

### 2009 -- Grindr
- **Founded by:** Joel Simkhai
- **Significance:** First location-based mobile dating app. Used GPS to connect nearby gay men in real-time. Demonstrated the power of geolocation + mobile for dating.
- **Ownership journey:** Sold 60% to Chinese company Kunlun Tech for $93 million (2016) --> Sold entirely for $600 million to San Vicente Acquisition Partners (2020) --> SPAC IPO in 2022 at $2.1 billion valuation.
- **2024 stats:** 13.5 million MAU, $345 million revenue.

### 2012 -- Tinder
- **Created at:** Hatch Labs hackathon (February 2012), an IAC-backed startup incubator
- **Founders:** Sean Rad, Joe Munoz (engineer), Justin Mateen, Whitney Wolfe Herd, and others
- **Key innovation:** The swipe interface (right for yes, left for no) combined with double opt-in matching. Gamified dating and made it feel casual and fun rather than serious and stigmatized.
- **Growth strategy:** Launched at USC via exclusive parties requiring app download. 400,000 users by early 2013. Top-grossing app in 99 countries by 2015. Highest-grossing app in Apple's App Store by 2017.
- **2024 stats:** 60 million MAU, 9.6 million subscribers, $1.94 billion revenue.

### 2012 -- Coffee Meets Bagel
- **Founded by:** Arum, Dawoon, and Soo Kang (three Korean-American sisters)
- **Significance:** Introduced curated, limited daily matches ("bagels") to combat choice overload. Famously turned down Mark Cuban's $30 million offer on Shark Tank in 2015.

### 2014 -- Bumble
- **Founded by:** Whitney Wolfe Herd and Andrey Andreev (founder of Badoo)
- **Key innovation:** Women-first messaging -- women must send the first message within 24 hours or the match expires. Used the familiar swipe interface but with a feminist positioning.
- **IPO:** February 2021. Wolfe Herd became the youngest female self-made billionaire at age 31.
- **2024 stats:** $866 million revenue, 14% market share.

---

## Era 3: Consolidation & Maturation (2016-2022)

### 2016 -- Hinge Relaunches as "Designed to Be Deleted"
- **Originally founded:** 2012 by Justin McLeod; launched February 2013
- **The pivot:** In October 2016, McLeod scrapped the original swiping app and relaunched with a completely new design focused on serious relationships. Users like specific parts of profiles instead of swiping. Introduced "Prompts" for personality showcase.
- **Result:** Became the fastest-growing dating app in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia. Acquired by Match Group in 2018.
- **2024 stats:** $550 million revenue, 8.9% market share.

### 2020 -- Match Group Spins Off from IAC
Match Group completed its separation from parent company IAC, becoming a fully independent public company (NASDAQ: MTCH).

### 2020 -- COVID-19 Transforms Dating
Major apps rapidly added video calling, voice notes, and virtual speed-dating features. Online dating became more mainstream and socially acceptable during lockdowns. Dating profiles became a "lifeline" for singles stuck at home.

### 2021 -- Bumble IPO
Bumble Inc. went public, valuing the company significantly. Whitney Wolfe Herd became a billionaire.

### 2022 -- Grindr SPAC IPO
Grindr went public via SPAC merger on NYSE at a $2.1 billion valuation.

---

## Era 4: Decline, AI, and Reinvention (2023-Present)

### 2024-2025 -- Industry-Wide Decline
- Match Group paying users fell 5% YoY to 13.8 million (Q4 2025)
- Tinder subscribers dropped 8% YoY
- Bumble paying users dropped 16% to 3.6 million (Q3 2025)
- 69% of dating apps deleted within one month of download (2025)
- Bumble stock fell from ~$75 (2021 IPO) to under $5 (March 2025)

### 2025-2026 -- The AI Pivot
- Tinder committed $60 million to AI and product development
- Bumble announced a complete platform rebuild as AI-first (target: mid-2026)
- Hinge introduced AI recommendation features (15% increase in matches)
- Justin McLeod (Hinge founder) left to start Overtone, an AI dating startup backed by Match Group

### 2026 -- In-Person Events Resurgence
44% increase in singles attending curated offline dating events since 2025. Apps are adding IRL event features alongside digital matching.
