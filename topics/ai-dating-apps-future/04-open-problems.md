# Open Problems in AI-Powered Dating Apps

## 1. Fake Profiles, Catfishing, and AI-Generated Deception

### The Problem

AI has transformed catfishing from a manual effort into an industrial-scale threat. Bad actors can now generate photorealistic faces, write convincing bios, and sustain AI-powered conversations — making detection far harder than before.

### Key Statistics
- **60%** of dating app users believe they've had a conversation written by AI
- **27%** have seen their own photos used by someone else's profile
- **50%** of Americans who used a dating app in the past 5 years experienced catfishing — double the rate from 5 years ago
- **Less than 46%** of people can correctly identify an AI-generated image (coin-flip accuracy)
- **GPT-4.5** fooled human judges 73% of the time in a 2025 preprint study
- **26%** of US singles now use AI to enhance dating (333% increase YoY)

### The "Chatfishing" Phenomenon

A new category beyond catfishing: users employ AI to write their messages while maintaining their real identity. This creates a disconnect where matches are "engaging while texting but boring in person." Tools like Rizz (~1.5M monthly users), YourMove (1M+ downloads), and built-in platform AI all facilitate this.

### Detection Approaches
- **Bumble's Deception Detector**: Blocks 95% of spam/scam accounts; reduced reports by 45% in 2 months
- **ChatFish App**: Third-party AI detector for dating apps; analyzes chat screenshots and photos
- **Norton Genie**: Free AI-powered scam detection for messages and links
- **Biometric verification**: Deepfake detection scanning profile images and video calls
- **Emerging ideas**: Crowd reporting, blockchain verification, AI deepfake detection working together

### Why It Remains Unsolved
- AI generation improves faster than detection
- Verification systems can be fooled (Reddit users report verified fake profiles on Bumble)
- Privacy-verification tradeoff: strong verification requires sensitive data (a dating app leaked 72,000 selfies; Discord lost 70,000 ID photos in October 2025)
- "Chatfishing" sits in a gray area — is AI-assisted messaging deception or just a tool?

## 2. Ghosting and Disposability Culture

### The Problem

Ghosting — abruptly cutting off communication without explanation — has become normalized on dating apps due to the absence of social accountability and the abundance of alternatives.

### Key Statistics
- **30%** of US adults have been ghosted
- **42%** of young adults (18-29) have been ghosted
- **62%** of active dating app users have been ghosted
- **~50%** of matches never respond at all
- **43%** of men's matches result in 0-1 messages

### Root Causes
1. **No mutual social connections**: Unlike meeting through friends, there's zero social cost to disappearing
2. **Paradox of choice**: Endless alternatives make any single connection feel disposable
3. **Maximizer mindset**: Users avoid committing because "someone better" may be a swipe away
4. **Low investment threshold**: Matching requires minimal effort, so matches feel low-value
5. **Gamification**: The interface treats people as cards to collect rather than humans to engage with

### Proposed Solutions (Largely Untested at Scale)
- Anti-ghosting features (app "After" includes this)
- Rating/feedback systems (like Airbnb's mutual review model)
- Match limits to increase investment per match
- Enforced response windows
- Social accountability through mutual friend connections
- Professional matchmaking as an alternative

### Why It Remains Unsolved
The fundamental tension: apps profit from engagement (lots of matches, lots of swiping), but ghosting is a natural consequence of that high-volume model. Solving ghosting requires reducing volume, which conflicts with the business model.

## 3. Gender Imbalance and the Male Experience Crisis

### The Problem

Dating apps have dramatically skewed gender ratios that create vastly different experiences for men and women, contributing to frustration, disillusionment, and mental health impacts.

### Key Statistics
- **Tinder**: 76% male users (3:1 ratio)
- **Bumble**: 67.4% male users (2:1 ratio)
- **Happn**: 60% male users
- **Ashley Madison**: 70%+ male users
- **63%** of men under 30 identify as single vs. **34%** of women (Pew Research)

### The Winner-Take-All Dynamic
- Women "like" only 14% of profiles; men swipe right 46% of the time
- The bottom 10% of women (15.87% match rate) outperform the top 10% of men (12.5%)
- Women accumulate 69% more total matches despite swiping 7.4x less
- Average male match rates are rising, but median is falling — top performers do better, most men fall further behind
- Swipe-match rates for men hit historic lows, often below 1%

### Impact
- **51%** of American men had zero dates in 2025
- Men's match rates peak at 18-21 (7.54%) and decline through the 30s
- Young male usage on major apps dropped 15-22% from 2023-2025
- Links to male loneliness epidemic: men under 40 feel trapped in app-based dating with no alternatives
- Dating app use correlated with heightened anxiety and depression vs. non-users

### Why It Remains Unsolved
- More women choosing to be single (64% refusing to settle)
- Men's higher desperation leads to less selective swiping, which further devalues their profiles
- Algorithms may amplify the imbalance by boosting "popular" profiles (ELO-like systems)
- Cultural factors: men are socialized away from emotional vulnerability, reducing relationship skills
- No incentive for platforms to fix it — the frustrated male user base drives subscription revenue

## 4. The Paradox of Choice

### The Problem

Barry Schwartz's research on choice overload is devastatingly applicable to dating apps: too many options lead to decision paralysis, decreased satisfaction, and perpetual dissatisfaction.

### How It Manifests
- **Shallow evaluation**: Overwhelmed brains use appearance-based heuristics instead of deep assessment
- **Decision paralysis**: Users endlessly browse rather than commit to meeting anyone
- **Post-choice regret**: Even after matching, users wonder if someone better is available
- **Reduced investment**: Why invest deeply in one person when hundreds more are available?
- **"Maximizer" vs. "satisficer" problem**: Maximizers (those who must find the "best" option) are perpetually unsatisfied

### Evidence
- Columbia University research confirms more options = less satisfying decisions
- Curated, verification-based communities deliver 2.3x higher long-term satisfaction (McKinsey Digital, 2023)
- A person's "essence" is reduced to photos and a brief bio judged in seconds — character, values, humor, and emotional intelligence cannot be evaluated through the interface

### Proposed Solutions
- Match limits (Hinge, Coffee Meets Bagel model)
- Curated recommendations over infinite browsing
- AI-driven pre-screening to reduce the pool to genuinely compatible options
- Enforced "slow dating" mechanics
- Shifting from "marketplace" to "matchmaker" UX paradigm

## 5. The AI Authenticity Paradox

### The Problem

AI is simultaneously positioned as the solution to dating app problems AND a major new source of those problems.

### The Paradox
- AI improves matching, safety, and user experience
- But AI also enables chatfishing, deepfakes, and inauthentic interaction
- Users want smarter AI matching but authentic human connection
- 80% of men and 90% of women doubt AI will produce more successful relationships
- 60% believe AI will pick partners within a decade

### Specific Tensions
1. **AI wingmen vs. authenticity**: If both users have AI writing their messages, who is actually connecting?
2. **AI companions vs. human relationships**: "AI girlfriend" searches up 525% YoY; 100M+ users interact with AI companions globally
3. **Efficiency vs. serendipity**: Better algorithms reduce the uncertainty and surprise that makes romance exciting
4. **Safety vs. privacy**: AI verification requires data collection that creates new attack surfaces

## 6. Mental Health and the Loneliness Feedback Loop

### The Problem

Dating apps were supposed to connect people, but growing evidence suggests they may be exacerbating loneliness and mental health issues.

### Key Data
- US Surgeon General declared loneliness an epidemic in 2023
- 85% of British Gen Z report feeling lonely (Hinge survey)
- 16% of men and 15% of women report feeling lonely "all or most of the time" (Pew, 2025)
- Men have 50% fewer close friendships than 20 years ago
- Dating app use linked to heightened anxiety and depression
- Ghosting linked to poorer life satisfaction and increased loneliness

### The Feedback Loop
1. User feels lonely → joins dating app
2. Experiences rejection, ghosting, low match rates → feels worse
3. Continues using app (addictive design) → burnout increases
4. Either leaves (contributing to user decline) or stays (contributing to mental health decline)

### Industry Response (Limited)
- Hinge: $1M fund for social groups and IRL events
- Bumble IRL: In-person events
- Some apps adding wellness features and mental health resources
- But fundamental model still prioritizes engagement over well-being

## 7. Monetization vs. User Satisfaction

### The Problem

The freemium model creates perverse incentives: apps profit most from users who are engaged but NOT finding lasting relationships.

### Evidence
- Paywalling basic features (seeing who liked you, unlimited swipes) frustrates users
- Subscription revenue drops when users find partners and leave
- Alleged practices: showing same profiles repeatedly, throttling matches for free users
- Only 18% of app-formed relationships lead to long-term commitments (2+ years)
- Only 12% of couples who met on apps end up married
- 60% of online relationships end within 6 months

### The Fundamental Conflict
The business model rewards retention and engagement. A perfectly successful dating app would lose all its users as they find partners. This creates an inherent tension between user satisfaction and revenue growth that no company has fully resolved.
