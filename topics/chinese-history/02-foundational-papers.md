# Foundational Works and Key Figures in Chinese History

## I. Essential Survey Histories

### The Harvard History of Imperial China (6 volumes)
The best starting point for serious readers. Each volume is ~300 pages, well-written, and incorporates the latest scholarship.

1. **Mark Edward Lewis, *The Early Chinese Empires: Qin and Han*** (2007)
2. **Mark Edward Lewis, *China Between Empires: The Northern and Southern Dynasties*** (2009)
3. **Mark Edward Lewis, *China's Cosmopolitan Empire: The Tang Dynasty*** (2009)
4. **Dieter Kuhn, *The Age of Confucian Rule: The Song Transformation of China*** (2009)
5. **Timothy Brook, *The Troubled Empire: China in the Yuan and Ming Dynasties*** (2010)
6. **William T. Rowe, *China's Last Empire: The Great Qing*** (2009)

### The Cambridge History of China (15+ volumes)
The gold standard for academic reference. Each volume is 800-1000+ pages. Key volumes:
- Vol. 1: *The Ch'in and Han Empires, 221 BC-AD 220* (ed. Twitchett & Loewe, 1986)
- Vol. 7: *The Ming Dynasty, 1368-1644* Part 1 (ed. Mote & Twitchett, 1988)
- Vol. 10: *Late Ch'ing 1800-1911* Part 1 (ed. Fairbank, 1978)
- Vol. 14-15: *The People's Republic* (ed. MacFarquhar & Fairbank, 1987/1991)

### Single-Volume Surveys
- **Patricia Buckley Ebrey, *The Cambridge Illustrated History of China*** (2nd ed., 2010) — Best single-volume overview, beautifully illustrated, balanced across all periods. Ideal for beginners.
- **John King Fairbank & Merle Goldman, *China: A New History*** (2nd ed., 2006) — The standard one-volume survey used in American universities for decades. Strong on modern period.
- **Jonathan Spence, *The Search for Modern China*** (3rd ed., 2012) — The definitive modern China survey (late Ming to present). Literary style, unusually engaging. ~728 pages.
- **Jacques Gernet, *A History of Chinese Civilization*** (2nd ed., 1996) — Comprehensive French-perspective survey. Strong on cultural and intellectual history.
- **Valerie Hansen, *The Open Empire: A History of China to 1800*** (2nd ed., 2015) — Emphasizes China's connections with the outside world. Challenges isolation narratives.
- **Li Feng, *Early China: A Social and Cultural History*** (2013) — Most current comprehensive survey of ancient China (Neolithic to Qin). Incorporates latest archaeology.

## II. Classical Chinese Historians

### Sima Qian (司马迁, c. 145-86 BCE)
- **Work**: *Records of the Grand Historian* (史记, Shiji)
- 130 chapters covering from the Yellow Emperor to Emperor Wu of Han (~2,000 years)
- Established the format for all subsequent Chinese dynastic histories: Basic Annals, Chronological Tables, Treatises, Hereditary Houses, Biographies
- Called the "father of Chinese historiography"
- Castrated by Emperor Wu for defending a defeated general; chose to endure to complete his life's work
- **English translation**: Burton Watson (Columbia University Press, multiple volumes)

### Ban Gu (班固, 32-92 CE)
- **Work**: *Book of Han* (汉书, Hanshu)
- First dynastic history to cover a single dynasty (Western Han)
- Established the "standard history" (正史) template used for all 24 official dynastic histories

### Sima Guang (司马光, 1019-1086)
- **Work**: *Comprehensive Mirror to Aid in Government* (资治通鉴, Zizhi Tongjian)
- Chronological history covering 1,362 years (403 BCE-959 CE), compiled over 19 years
- The greatest work of Chinese chronological history

## III. Period-Specific Foundational Works

### Ancient China
- **Michael Loewe & Edward Shaughnessy (eds.), *The Cambridge History of Ancient China*** (1999) — Authoritative academic reference for pre-imperial China
- **David Keightley, *Sources of Shang History: The Oracle-Bone Inscriptions of Bronze Age China*** (1978) — Foundational work on oracle bones
- **Lothar von Falkenhausen, *Chinese Society in the Age of Confucius*** (2006) — Archaeological approach to Zhou society

### Tang Dynasty
- **Stephen Owen, *The Great Age of Chinese Poetry: The High T'ang*** (1981) — Essential for Tang poetry
- **Lewis, *China's Cosmopolitan Empire*** (2009)

### Song Dynasty
- **Jacques Gernet, *Daily Life in China on the Eve of the Mongol Invasion, 1250-1276*** (1962) — Classic social history
- **Peter Bol, *"This Culture of Ours": Intellectual Transitions in T'ang and Sung China*** (1992)

### Yuan-Ming
- **Morris Rossabi, *Khubilai Khan: His Life and Times*** (1988)
- **Timothy Brook, *The Confusions of Pleasure: Commerce and Culture in Ming China*** (1998)
- **Ray Huang, *1587, A Year of No Significance*** (1981) — Innovative microhistory showing how a "nothing happened" year reveals systemic Ming problems

### Qing Dynasty
- **Jonathan Spence, *Emperor of China: Self-Portrait of K'ang-hsi*** (1974) — Innovative biography using Kangxi's own words
- **Mark Elliott, *The Manchu Way*** (2001) — Transformative "New Qing History" work
- **Julia Lovell, *The Opium War*** (2011)
- **Stephen Platt, *Autumn in the Heavenly Kingdom*** (2012) — Taiping Rebellion narrative history

### Modern China (1912-1949)
- **Rana Mitter, *Forgotten Ally: China's World War II, 1937-1945*** (2013)
- **Rana Mitter, *A Bitter Revolution: China's Struggle with the Modern World*** (2004)

### Mao Era
- **Frank Dikötter's "People's Trilogy"**:
  - *The Tragedy of Liberation* (2013) — 1945-1957
  - *Mao's Great Famine* (2010) — 1958-1962 (estimates 45 million dead)
  - *The Cultural Revolution* (2016) — 1962-1976
- **Roderick MacFarquhar, *The Origins of the Cultural Revolution*** (3 vols., 1974-1997)
- **Yang Jisheng, *Tombstone*** (2012 English ed.) — Great Famine, by a Chinese journalist using internal party documents

### Reform Era and Contemporary
- **Ezra Vogel, *Deng Xiaoping and the Transformation of China*** (2011) — Definitive biography
- **Richard McGregor, *The Party: The Secret World of China's Communist Rulers*** (2010) — How the CCP actually works
- **Elizabeth Economy, *The Third Revolution: Xi Jinping and the New Chinese State*** (2018)
- **Peter Hessler's trilogy**: *River Town* (2001), *Oracle Bones* (2006), *Country Driving* (2010) — Immersive narrative nonfiction

### The Great Divergence
- **Kenneth Pomeranz, *The Great Divergence*** (2000) — Why Europe industrialized and China didn't. Argues China and Europe were comparable until ~1750.
- **Mark Elvin, *The Retreat of the Elephants*** (2004) — Environmental history of China over 4,000 years

## V. Key Modern Historians

| Historian | Contribution |
|-----------|-------------|
| **John King Fairbank** (1907-1991) | Founded modern Chinese studies in America (Harvard). His "impact-response" model dominated for decades. |
| **Jonathan Spence** (1936-2021) | Master narrator of modern Chinese history. *The Search for Modern China* is the standard text. |
| **Joseph Needham** (1900-1995) | *Science and Civilisation in China* (27 vols.) — monumental documentation of Chinese scientific achievement. Posed the "Needham Question": why didn't China develop modern science? |
| **Gu Jiegang** (1893-1980) | Founded the "Doubting Antiquity" school — systematically questioned legendary figures |
| **Chen Yinke** (1890-1969) | Often considered the greatest Chinese historian of the 20th century. Medieval China specialist. |
| **Qian Mu** (1895-1990) | Conservative Confucian historian. *Outline of Chinese History* was the standard Chinese university textbook. |
| **Ray Huang** (1918-2000) | Pioneered "macro-history" approach. Argued China's core challenge was building effective fiscal systems. |
| **Kenneth Pomeranz** (b. 1958) | *The Great Divergence* — transformed comparative economic history |
| **Mark Edward Lewis** (b. 1954) | Three volumes of the Harvard series; leading scholar of early imperial China |

## VI. Academic Resources

### Journals
- **The Journal of Asian Studies** — Broadest scope
- **Harvard Journal of Asiatic Studies** — One of the oldest and most prestigious
- **The China Quarterly** — Premier journal for contemporary China
- **T'oung Pao** (since 1890) — Classical sinology
- **Late Imperial China** — Ming-Qing specialist
- **Early China** — Pre-imperial specialist

### Online Courses
- **Harvard ChinaX (edX)** — Free multi-part course taught by Peter Bol and William Kirby. 10 modules, ancient to contemporary.
- **Chinese Text Project (ctext.org)** — Free digital library of pre-modern Chinese texts with English translations
- **China Biographical Database (CBDB)** — Harvard/Academia Sinica, ~427,000 biographical records

### Documentaries
- **China: A Century of Revolution** (PBS, 3 parts, 1989-1997) — Gold standard for 20th-century China
- **The Story of China** (BBC, 2016) — Michael Wood's 6-part series covering all periods
- **Morning Sun** (2003) — Cultural Revolution documentary

### Podcasts
- **The China History Podcast** (Laszlo Montgomery) — 300+ episodes, ancient to modern
