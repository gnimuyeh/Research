# Learning Path: Chinese History from Origins to Present

## How to Use This Guide

This is a progressive curriculum designed so a motivated beginner can build comprehensive knowledge of Chinese history. Each stage builds on the previous one. Estimated total time: 12-18 months of dedicated reading.

---

## Stage 1: Get the Big Picture (2-3 weeks)

### Goal
Understand the full sweep of Chinese history at a high level — the major dynasties, key turning points, and overarching themes.

### Read
- **Patricia Buckley Ebrey, *The Cambridge Illustrated History of China*** (384 pages) — The best single-volume entry point

### Watch
- **The Story of China** (BBC, 2016) — Michael Wood's 6-part documentary covering all periods
- **Harvard ChinaX** (edX) — Free online course, Modules 1-2 (overview)

### Key Questions to Answer
- What are the major dynasties in order? What are the main periods of unity vs. fragmentation?
- What is the Mandate of Heaven and the dynastic cycle?
- What role does Confucianism play across Chinese history?
- What is the "Century of Humiliation" and why does it matter today?

---

## Stage 2: Ancient Foundations — Origins through Han (4-6 weeks)

### Goal
Understand the origins of Chinese civilization, the first empires, and the philosophical foundations that shaped everything after.

### Read (in order)
1. **Li Feng, *Early China: A Social and Cultural History*** (2013) — The most current archaeological/historical survey of ancient China
2. **Mark Edward Lewis, *The Early Chinese Empires: Qin and Han*** (2007) — Qin unification through Han dynasty
3. **Sima Qian, *Records of the Grand Historian*** — Read selections (Burton Watson translation). At minimum: the biographies of Qin Shi Huang, Xiang Yu, and the founding of the Han

### Primary Sources
- *Analects of Confucius* (Slingerland translation) — selections
- *Dao De Jing* (D.C. Lau translation)
- *Art of War* (any good translation)

### Key Topics
- Neolithic cultures (Yangshao, Longshan, Liangzhu) and the multi-regional origins of Chinese civilization
- Shang oracle bones and the birth of Chinese writing
- The Hundred Schools of Thought: Confucianism, Daoism, Legalism, Mohism
- Qin unification and its lasting institutional legacy
- Han Dynasty: Confucianism as state ideology, the Silk Road, Sima Qian
- The "Han Chinese" identity and why it endures

### Explore
- Visit [ctext.org](https://ctext.org) — read original texts with English translations
- Explore the Sanxingdui discoveries (2020-2022) — challenges the Yellow River-centric narrative

---

## Stage 3: The Middle Empire — Disunity through Tang (3-4 weeks)

### Goal
Understand how China fragmented, absorbed Buddhism, and rebuilt into the cosmopolitan Tang golden age.

### Read
1. **Mark Edward Lewis, *China Between Empires*** (2009) — Period of Disunity (220-589)
2. **Mark Edward Lewis, *China's Cosmopolitan Empire: The Tang Dynasty*** (2009)

### Key Topics
- Three Kingdoms — history vs. the novel *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*
- Buddhism's spread in China and its transformation (Pure Land, Chan/Zen)
- The Sui Grand Canal — why it matters for all subsequent Chinese history
- Wu Zetian — China's only female emperor
- Tang Chang'an as the world's largest, most cosmopolitan city
- Tang poetry: Li Bai, Du Fu, Wang Wei, Bai Juyi
- The An Lushan Rebellion (755-763) — one of the deadliest conflicts in human history

### Primary Sources
- Read Tang poetry: Start with Li Bai's "Quiet Night Thought" and Du Fu's war poetry
- *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* — read abridged version or key chapters

---

## Stage 4: Commercial Revolution and Conquest — Song through Yuan (3-4 weeks)

### Goal
Understand China's economic and technological golden age and its devastating Mongol conquest.

### Read
1. **Dieter Kuhn, *The Age of Confucian Rule*** (2009) — Song Dynasty
2. **Timothy Brook, *The Troubled Empire*** (2010) — Yuan and Ming

### Key Topics
- Song economic revolution: paper money, proto-industrialization, urbanization
- The "Four Great Inventions" — printing, compass, gunpowder, paper
- Neo-Confucianism (Zhu Xi) and its 600-year dominance of Chinese intellectual life
- Military weakness: Song's chronic inability to defend its northern border
- Mongol conquest — Genghis Khan to Kublai Khan
- What Mongol rule meant for Chinese identity and governance

### Supplementary
- Jacques Gernet, *Daily Life in China on the Eve of the Mongol Invasion* — vivid social history

---

## Stage 5: Restoration and Isolation — Ming Dynasty (2-3 weeks)

### Goal
Understand the Ming's restoration of Han Chinese rule, Zheng He's voyages, and the fateful turn toward isolation.

### Read
1. Continue with **Timothy Brook, *The Troubled Empire*** (2010)
2. **Ray Huang, *1587, A Year of No Significance*** (1981) — short, brilliant microhistory

### Key Topics
- Zhu Yuanzhang: peasant to emperor
- The Forbidden City and Zheng He's voyages (1405-1433)
- Why China turned inward — the maritime withdrawal debate
- The Great Wall as we know it (Ming construction)
- Wang Yangming's rival Neo-Confucianism
- Late Ming crisis: Little Ice Age, fiscal collapse, peasant rebellion
- The Ming novel tradition: *Journey to the West*, *Water Margin*, *Romance of Three Kingdoms*

---

## Stage 6: The Last Empire — Qing Dynasty (3-4 weeks)

### Goal
Understand Manchu rule, the High Qing prosperity, and the catastrophic 19th-century collapse.

### Read
1. **William Rowe, *China's Last Empire: The Great Qing*** (2009)
2. **Jonathan Spence, *Emperor of China: Self-Portrait of K'ang-hsi*** (1974) — optional but excellent
3. **Stephen Platt, *Autumn in the Heavenly Kingdom*** (2012) — the Taiping Rebellion

### Key Topics
- Manchu conquest: the queue order and ethnic governance
- Kangxi-Yongzheng-Qianlong: the "High Qing" golden age
- Qing expansion: Tibet, Xinjiang, Mongolia, Taiwan
- Population explosion (150M → 450M) and its consequences
- The Opium Wars and unequal treaties
- Taiping Rebellion — the deadliest civil war in history (20-30 million dead)
- Self-Strengthening Movement, Hundred Days' Reform, Boxer Rebellion
- Empress Dowager Cixi
- 1911 Revolution and the end of 2,000 years of imperial rule

### The Great Divergence
- Read or survey **Kenneth Pomeranz, *The Great Divergence*** (2000) — why didn't China industrialize first?

---

## Stage 7: Revolution and Republic — 1912-1949 (3-4 weeks)

### Goal
Understand the tumultuous Republican era: warlords, intellectual revolution, war, and Communist victory.

### Read
1. **Jonathan Spence, *The Search for Modern China*** (2012) — Chapters covering 1912-1949
2. **Rana Mitter, *Forgotten Ally*** (2013) — China's WWII experience

### Key Topics
- Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles and Yuan Shikai's failed monarchy
- May Fourth Movement (1919) — China's intellectual revolution
- CCP founding (1921) and the First United Front
- Shanghai Massacre (1927) — Chiang Kai-shek turns on the Communists
- The Long March (1934-1935) and Mao's rise
- Nanjing Massacre (1937) and the war against Japan
- Why the Communists won the Civil War

---

## Stage 8: Mao's China — 1949-1976 (3-4 weeks)

### Goal
Understand the radical transformations, catastrophic campaigns, and human costs of the Mao era.

### Read
1. **Frank Dikötter, *Mao's Great Famine*** (2010) — The Great Leap Forward
2. **Frank Dikötter, *The Cultural Revolution*** (2016)
3. Continue Spence's *Search for Modern China* for narrative context

### Key Topics
- Land reform and class struggle (1949-1953)
- Korean War
- Hundred Flowers → Anti-Rightist Campaign (1956-1958)
- Great Leap Forward and the Great Famine (30-45 million dead)
- Sino-Soviet split
- Cultural Revolution: Red Guards, destruction, sent-down youth, Gang of Four
- Nixon's visit (1972)
- Mao's death (1976) and aftermath

### Watch
- **China: A Century of Revolution** (PBS) — Parts 2 and 3

---

## Stage 9: Reform and Rise — 1976-2012 (2-3 weeks)

### Goal
Understand China's transformation from impoverished backwater to the world's second-largest economy.

### Read
1. **Ezra Vogel, *Deng Xiaoping and the Transformation of China*** (2011)
2. **Peter Hessler, *River Town*** (2001) — experience of China in the late 1990s

### Key Topics
- Deng Xiaoping's "Reform and Opening Up" (1978)
- Special Economic Zones and Shenzhen's transformation
- One-child policy and its long-term consequences
- Democracy Wall and Wei Jingsheng
- Tiananmen Square (1989) — what happened and its lasting impact
- Jiang Zemin, WTO accession (2001), and economic takeoff
- Hu Jintao, Beijing Olympics (2008), world's second-largest economy (2010)
- The "grand bargain": prosperity in exchange for political compliance

---

## Stage 10: Xi Jinping's China — 2012-Present (2-3 weeks)

### Goal
Understand contemporary China's trajectory, tensions, and uncertain future.

### Read
1. **Elizabeth Economy, *The Third Revolution*** (2018)
2. **Richard McGregor, *The Party*** (2010) — how the CCP works
3. Current journalism: *The Economist*, *Financial Times*, *South China Morning Post*

### Key Topics
- Anti-corruption campaign
- Belt and Road Initiative
- Abolition of term limits (2018)
- Hong Kong: Umbrella Movement → 2019 protests → National Security Law
- Xinjiang/Uyghur situation
- COVID-19: zero-COVID and its reversal
- US-China tensions: trade war, tech decoupling, Taiwan
- Economic challenges: property crisis, youth unemployment, demographic decline
- China's AI and technology ambitions
