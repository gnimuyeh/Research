# Ancient Chinese History: Origins to Han Dynasty — Overview

## What Is This Field?

Ancient Chinese history covers the span from the earliest evidence of human habitation in China (roughly 800,000–700,000 BCE) through the fall of the Han Dynasty in 220 CE — a period of nearly a million years that includes the emergence of agriculture, writing, philosophy, empire, and one of humanity's most enduring civilizations.

The field draws on archaeology, epigraphy (especially oracle bone inscriptions), transmitted classical texts, physical anthropology, genetics, and comparative linguistics. Because China maintained continuous literary traditions, it is one of the few ancient civilizations where archaeological evidence can be cross-referenced with detailed written records dating back over 3,000 years.

## The Big Questions

1. **How did Chinese civilization emerge?** Was it a single-origin development along the Yellow River, or a mosaic of regional cultures that converged? Modern archaeology now strongly supports the multi-regional model.

2. **Did the Xia Dynasty exist?** Traditional historiography names Xia as China's first dynasty (~2070–1600 BCE), but no contemporary written records survive. The Erlitou archaeological site (~1900–1600 BCE) shows a complex early state, but the equation Erlitou = Xia remains debated between Chinese and Western scholars.

3. **How did Chinese writing develop?** Oracle bone script (c. 1250–1046 BCE) is the oldest confirmed Chinese writing, but its sophistication suggests earlier antecedents. What are they?

4. **What caused the Axial Age flourishing?** Why did the political chaos of the Warring States period (475–221 BCE) produce the richest philosophical outpouring in Chinese history — Confucianism, Daoism, Legalism, Mohism, and more?

5. **Why did the Qin Dynasty collapse so quickly?** Despite unifying China and creating its first true empire in 221 BCE, Qin lasted only 15 years. What structural forces made it so unstable?

6. **How did Han shape Chinese identity?** The majority ethnic group of China today still calls itself "Han" after a dynasty that ended in 220 CE. What made the Han's cultural synthesis so durable?

## Why This Field Matters

- China's civilization is one of humanity's oldest continuous cultural traditions, with unbroken literary and institutional memory stretching back over 3,000 years.
- The philosophical schools of this era — especially Confucianism, Daoism, and Legalism — shaped governance, family structure, education, and ethics across East Asia for two millennia and remain influential today.
- The Han Dynasty established a template of imperial governance that persisted, with modifications, until 1912 CE.
- Understanding ancient China is essential context for understanding modern China: its political culture, its conception of legitimacy, its concept of national identity, and its relationship with history.

## Chronological Outline

| Period | Dates | Key Markers |
|---|---|---|
| Paleolithic / Early Humans | ~800,000–10,000 BCE | Peking Man at Zhoukoudian; stone tools |
| Neolithic | ~7000–1700 BCE | Agriculture, pottery, jade, proto-urban settlements |
| Legendary Period | ~2852–2070 BCE (traditional) | Three Sovereigns, Five Emperors; mythological founders |
| Xia Dynasty (semi-legendary) | ~2070–1600 BCE | Erlitou culture; first bronze casting; debated historicity |
| Shang Dynasty | ~1600–1046 BCE | Oracle bone writing; Bronze Age apex; Yinxu capital |
| Western Zhou | ~1046–771 BCE | Mandate of Heaven; feudal (fengjian) system |
| Spring and Autumn Period | 771–476 BCE | Political fragmentation; Confucius; early iron age |
| Warring States Period | 476–221 BCE | Hundred Schools of Thought; mass warfare; iron weapons |
| Qin Dynasty | 221–206 BCE | First unified empire; standardization; Terracotta Army |
| Western Han | 206 BCE–9 CE | Silk Road; Confucian state ideology; Sima Qian |
| Xin Dynasty (Wang Mang) | 9–23 CE | Reform interlude; social upheaval |
| Eastern Han | 25–220 CE | Restoration; paper invention; Yellow Turban Rebellion |
